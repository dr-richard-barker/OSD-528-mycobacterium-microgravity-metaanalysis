\section*{Introduction}

Bacteria in spacecraft grow in fluids where gravity no longer drives sedimentation or convection, and microgravity can change bacterial gene expression and behaviour\cite{nickerson2000microgravity}. Spaceflight experiments are scarce, so most of this biology is studied on the ground with microgravity simulators, which keep reorienting the sample so that the gravity vector averages towards zero. Simulators differ in design and cost, and whether they produce the same biological response is an open, practical question.

Non-tuberculous mycobacteria are a concern for engineered water systems because they persist in plumbing and form biofilms\cite{falkinham2015common}. \textit{Mycobacterium marinum}, a fish pathogen closely related to \textit{Mycobacterium tuberculosis}\cite{stinear2008insights}, can be handled at biosafety level~2 and grows either as a biofilm or in suspension, which makes it a convenient model\cite{clary2022development,osd528data}. Clary and colleagues built an inexpensive 3D clinostat and compared it with a commercial RPM~2.0 using \textit{M.~marinum}\cite{clary2022development}. They reported that the two machines produced similar attached biofilms and similar transcriptome changes in the bacteria in suspension, relative to normal gravity. Their RNA-seq data are openly available in NASA's Open Science Data Repository as study OSD-528\cite{osd528data}.

Here we re-analyse that deposit to put numbers on the comparison: how large each simulator's transcriptional response is, how far the two responses overlap, and what functions they involve. In doing so we found that one RPM library in the deposit duplicates another, and we describe how we handled it. We quantified every read with rRNA-aware pseudoalignment, tested differential expression with DESeq2, built a co-expression network, tested GO enrichment, mapped expression changes onto KEGG-supported metabolic reactions, and asked whether a tabular foundation model, TabPFN\cite{hollmann2025accurate}, can classify the conditions from eight samples better than simple baselines. All code and outputs are open, and every number in the text is generated from them\cite{wilkinson2016fair}.

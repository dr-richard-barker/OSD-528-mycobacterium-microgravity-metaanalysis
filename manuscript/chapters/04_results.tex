\section*{Results}

\subsection*{Read data and the RFPRPM4 deposit defect}
The \nSamplesDeposited{} deposited libraries contained \pairsMinM--\pairsMaxM{} million read pairs each. With the genome's non-coding RNA genes included as decoys, \pctAlignedMin--\pctAlignedMax\% of pairs pseudoaligned, but most of them to rRNA (\pctRrnaMin--\pctRrnaMax\%; Fig.~\ref{fig:fig1}b). Only \pctCdsMin--\pctCdsMax\% fell on protein-coding genes. The analysis therefore rests on \cdsPairsPrimaryMinK{}{,}000--\cdsPairsPrimaryMaxK{}{,}000 coding read pairs per primary sample: enough to quantify most genes, but thinner than the raw totals suggest.

The two FASTQ files of sample RFPRPM4 each start with a byte-identical copy of the corresponding RFPRPM41 file, followed by a second library with its own index (\rpmFourPairs{} read pairs; Methods). Earlier analyses of this deposit that read RFPRPM4 from the start would have counted RFPRPM41 twice. We excluded the inferred RFPRPM4 library from the primary analysis, leaving \nGround{} ground, \nClino{} clinostat and \nRpm{} RPM samples (Fig.~\ref{fig:fig1}a), and tested its effect separately.

\subsection*{Both simulators produce a large, shared transcriptional response}
Simulated microgravity dominated the variation between samples: PC1 explained \pcOnePct\% of the variance and separated the ground controls (PC1 $\pcOneGroundMin$ to $\pcOneGroundMax$) from all but one simulator sample ($\pcOneMgMin$ to $\pcOneMgMax$; Fig.~\ref{fig:fig2}a). The exception was clinostat sample RFP3D11, which lay between the two groups (PC1 $\pcOneThreeDElevenScore$). PC2 explained \pcTwoPct\% and did not separate the two simulators.

Of \nGenesTested{} genes tested, \nDegClino{} were differentially expressed under the clinostat (\nDegClinoUp{} up, \nDegClinoDown{} down) and \nDegRpm{} under the RPM (\nDegRpmUp{} up, \nDegRpmDown{} down; FDR $<0.05$; Fig.~\ref{fig:fig2}b,c). Down-regulation outnumbered up-regulation under both simulators. Almost all of these DEGs also changed at least two-fold after apeglm shrinkage (\nDegClinoLfcOne{} and \nDegRpmLfcOne).

The two responses largely coincide (Fig.~\ref{fig:fig8}). \nDegShared{} genes were DEGs under both simulators, all in the same direction (\nSharedUp{} up, \nSharedDown{} down), out of \nDegEither{} DEGs under either (Jaccard index \jaccardShared). Across all tested genes the two sets of fold changes correlated with Spearman $\rho=\rhoSimulators$. Comparing the simulators directly found \nDegClinoRpm{} DEGs. With only \nRpm{} RPM samples this comparison has little power, so it shows no detectable difference rather than proving the two responses identical.

Adding the inferred RFPRPM4 library (RPM $n=3$) increased the RPM DEG count to \nDegRpmSens{} and the clinostat count to \nDegClinoSens{}. It recovered \nDegRpmBothSens{} of the \nDegRpm{} primary RPM DEGs, with fold changes that correlated with the primary ones at Pearson $r=\rLfcRpmSens$. The clinostat-vs-RPM comparison again found \nDegClinoRpmSens{} DEGs (Supplementary Fig.~S1). The conclusions do not depend on how the defective deposit is handled.

\subsection*{Functional content of the shared response}
The GO analysis rests on electronic annotations (\goIeaPct\% IEA) covering \goAnnotatedPct\% of tested genes, so it describes predicted function. The shared up-regulated genes were enriched for the cellular component ``extracellular region'' (\goOverlapExtracellSharedUp{} genes, FDR $=\goFdrExtracellSharedUp$; Fig.~\ref{fig:fig5}); \goMceSharedUp{} of these genes encode Mce-family proteins. They were also enriched for NADH dehydrogenase activity (\goOverlapNadhSharedUp, FDR $=\goFdrNadhSharedUp$). This signal was strongest under the RPM, where \goOverlapNadhRpmUp{} annotated NADH dehydrogenase genes were up (FDR $=\goFdrNadhRpmUp$); all of them are subunits of NADH:quinone oxidoreductase (complex~I). The shared down-regulated genes were enriched for DNA-binding transcription factor activity (\goOverlapTfSharedDown, FDR $=\goFdrTfSharedDown$) and for the response to heat (\goOverlapHeatSharedDown, FDR $=\goFdrHeatSharedDown$): \goGenesHeatSharedDown. The leading terms (extracellular region, NADH dehydrogenase activity, transcription factor activity and response to heat) were each significant under both simulators separately; some weaker terms reached significance only in the larger shared sets (Fig.~\ref{fig:fig5}).

\subsection*{Co-expression modules}
WGCNA grouped \wgcnaGenes{} genes into \nModules{} modules (\nGrey{} unassigned; power \wgcnaPower{}, scale-free $R^2=\wgcnaRsq$; Fig.~\ref{fig:fig3}). Of \nModuleTraitTests{} module--trait correlations, \nModuleTraitSig{} passed FDR $<0.05$, both with simulated microgravity: the turquoise module (\sizeTurquoise{} genes, $r=\rTurquoise$, FDR $=\fdrTurquoise$) and the blue module (\sizeBlue{} genes, $r=\rBlue$, FDR $=\fdrBlue$). No module correlated significantly with either simulator on its own. The turquoise module is enriched for transcription factor activity and lipid metabolic process, and the blue module for NADH dehydrogenase activity and the respiratory chain (Supplementary Fig.~S2). They mirror the down- and up-regulated arms of the DEG analysis. Figure~\ref{fig:fig6} lists their most connected genes. With eight samples these hub rankings are fragile and are given as leads, not findings.

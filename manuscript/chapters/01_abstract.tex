\begin{abstract}
Ground-based microgravity simulators are widely used to study microbial responses to spaceflight, but whether different machines produce the same biological response is rarely tested quantitatively. We re-analysed NASA Open Science Data Repository study OSD-528, in which \textit{Mycobacterium marinum} was grown for four days on a lab-built 3D clinostat, a commercial RPM~2.0 or static 1g, and the bacteria in suspension were sequenced. The deposited files for one RPM library begin with a byte-identical copy of another library's files, so the primary analysis used \nGround{} ground, \nClino{} clinostat and \nRpm{} RPM samples. Every read was re-quantified with rRNA-aware pseudoalignment. Of \nGenesTested{} genes tested, \nDegClino{} were differentially expressed under the clinostat and \nDegRpm{} under the RPM (FDR $<0.05$). \nDegShared{} genes changed under both simulators, all in the same direction; genome-wide fold changes correlated at Spearman $\rho=\rhoSimulators$, and no gene differed significantly between the simulators. The shared response raised Mce-family lipid-transport genes and complex~I (\textit{nuo}) genes, and lowered transcription factors and heat-shock genes. Mycolic acid synthesis genes did not change. Co-expression analysis found two modules tracking simulated microgravity (\nModuleTraitSig{} of \nModuleTraitTests{} module--trait tests at FDR $<0.05$). Leave-one-out classifiers, including the tabular foundation model TabPFN, classified \mlBinTabpfnFiftyCorrect{} held-out samples correctly as simulated microgravity or 1g, which is not clearly better than chance with eight samples, and could not robustly separate the two simulators. The two simulators drove largely the same transcriptional programme, supporting the inexpensive clinostat as a substitute for the RPM in this system. All code, outputs and generated numbers are openly available.
\end{abstract}
